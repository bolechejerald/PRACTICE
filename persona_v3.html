ABOUT DARYL

Daryl relies on her smartphone for entertainment, staying connected with friends, and club work, and her laptop for school requirements, technology-assisted learning, and research. A visual impairment means long screen sessions without her glasses bring on digital eye strain, so she leans on high-contrast display settings and clearly organized layouts to keep using her devices comfortably.

👁

Accessibility need: Visual impairment — benefits from high-contrast display, medium-sized tap targets, and plain, step-by-step instructions.

WANTS & NEEDS
  • High-contrast mode for comfortable reading over long sessions.
  • Medium-sized buttons that are easy to tap accurately.
  • Well-organized themes and image/text combinations.
  • Simple, step-by-step instructions on forms and sign-ups.
  • Detailed layouts are fine, as long as they're organized.
WHERE SHE SPENDS TIME
  • TikTok, Netflix, Messenger, and X for daily connection.
  • Online reading and fan communities in her downtime.
  • Stepped away from a paid-per-chapter reading app.
  • Google Forms for school — when instructions are clear.
FRUSTRATIONS
  1. Digital eye strain after long use without glasses.
  2. Unclear or cluttered instructions, especially in forms.
  3. Apps that gate content behind per-chapter payments.
  4. Account recovery stalling with no one able to help.
GOALS & MOTIVATION
  1. Finish school requirements independently.
  2. Resolve her own account issues without depending on unreachable support.
  3. Feel confident in new apps because instructions are clear.

Small text and low-contrast screens are the hardest part. After a while without my glasses, letters start to blur together and my eyes get tired fast — I end up zooming in or squinting just to read a simple caption.

— DARYL, ON READING WITH A VISUAL IMPAIRMENT
